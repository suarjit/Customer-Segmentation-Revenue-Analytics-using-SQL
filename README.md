# Customer-Segmentation-Revenue-Analytics-using-SQL
# Chinook Customer Spending & Music Sales Analysis Using SQL

## 1. Project Objective

The objective of this project is to analyze customer purchasing behavior, revenue generation, and music sales performance using SQL in the Chinook database.

The project focuses on identifying high-value customers, frequent purchasers, inactive customers, popular music genres, top-performing artists, and best-selling tracks. It also examines customer spending patterns, country-wise customer rankings, revenue contributions, and cumulative monthly revenue.

The analysis uses SQL joins, aggregate functions, Common Table Expressions (CTEs), subqueries, conditional statements, and window functions to extract meaningful business insights and support data-driven decision-making.

## 2. Dataset

**Database Name:** `Chinook`

**Database Type:** Relational Database

The Chinook database contains information about customers, invoices, music tracks, artists, albums, and genres.

The project primarily uses the following tables:

| Table | Description |
|---|---|
| `Customer` | Contains customer details, including customer ID, name, and country. |
| `Invoice` | Stores invoice information, including customer ID, invoice date, and total amount. |
| `InvoiceLine` | Contains individual items purchased in each invoice, including track ID, quantity, and unit price. |
| `Track` | Contains track information and its associated album and genre. |
| `Genre` | Stores music genre information. |
| `Artist` | Contains artist names and artist IDs. |
| `Album` | Connects artists with their albums. |

These tables are joined using primary and foreign key relationships to analyze customer spending, purchasing frequency, music preferences, and sales performance.

## 3. Analysis Performed

The project consists of 15 SQL queries covering customer analytics, music sales, revenue analysis, and advanced SQL techniques.

### A. Customer Spending Analysis

- Identified the top 10 highest-spending customers based on total invoice amounts.
- Found the 10 most frequent purchasers by counting their invoices.
- Identified customers who had not purchased during the six months preceding the latest invoice date, as well as customers who had never purchased.
- Calculated average customer spending and total revenue.
- Identified customers whose total spending was below the average customer spending.

### B. Country-Wise Customer Analysis

- Compared countries based on average customer spending and total country revenue.
- Ranked customers by spending within each country using the `DENSE_RANK()` window function.
- Identified the top three customers in each country using `ROW_NUMBER()`.
- Examined customer spending patterns across different countries.

### C. Customer Segmentation

- Divided customers into three segments: High Value, Medium Value, and Low Value.
- Classified customers spending at least twice the average as High Value.
- Classified customers spending at least the average but less than twice the average as Medium Value.
- Classified customers spending below the average as Low Value.
- Included customers without invoices by using `LEFT JOIN` and `COALESCE()`.

### D. Music Genre Analysis

- Identified popular music genres based on the total quantity of tracks purchased.
- Calculated the number of unique customers purchasing each genre.
- Measured revenue generated by each genre.

### E. Artist and Track Performance

- Identified the top 10 artists by total revenue.
- Calculated the number of invoices, tracks sold, and revenue generated by each artist.
- Found the 10 most frequently purchased tracks.
- Compared track popularity and revenue generation.

### F. Customer Music Preferences

- Identified customers who purchased music from multiple genres.
- Counted the distinct genres purchased by each customer.
- Listed the genres associated with each customer's purchases.
- Calculated revenue generated by customers purchasing across multiple genres.

### G. Revenue Contribution Analysis

- Calculated each customer's total spending.
- Measured each customer's percentage contribution to total revenue.
- Ranked customers according to their revenue contribution.
- Used window functions to calculate the percentage share of total revenue.

### H. Monthly and Cumulative Revenue Analysis

- Calculated total revenue for each month.
- Used the `SUM()` window function to calculate cumulative revenue over time.
- Examined how monthly revenue contributes to the overall running total.

### I. Advanced SQL Techniques

The project demonstrates several important SQL concepts:

- **JOINs:** Combine related data from multiple tables.
- **GROUP BY and aggregate functions:** Calculate spending, revenue, invoice counts, and purchase quantities.
- **CTEs:** Organize complex queries into readable intermediate result sets.
- **Subqueries:** Compare customer spending with average spending.
- **CASE statements:** Create customer segments and customer activity labels.
- **Window functions:** Rank customers and calculate revenue percentages and cumulative totals.
- **DENSE_RANK() and ROW_NUMBER():** Rank customers within country groups.
- **COALESCE() and NULLIF():** Handle missing spending values and protect against division by zero.
